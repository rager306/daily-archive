A Unifying Lens on Supervised Fine-Tuning Through Target Distribution Design

Tong Xie1   Yuanhao Ban1,2   Yunqi Hong1   Sohyun An1   Yihang Chen1   Cho-Jui Hsieh1,2
1University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA),   2Arena
{tongxie,chohsieh}@cs.ucla.edu
Project Page:  Target-SFT
Abstract

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) typically maximizes the likelihood of every token in a demonstrated trajectory. However, an observed token can be non-unique, noisy, or misaligned with the model prior. Strictly fitting toward this one-hot target may be suboptimal, especially when the pretrained model encodes a rich knowledge prior. In this work, we reinterpret SFT as target distribution design: instead of studying only the loss objective, we analyze the token-level target that the loss drives the model to match. We introduce the QQ-target framework, which decomposes SFT supervision into two explicit choices: (1) how strongly to rely on the observed token, and (2) how to allocate the remaining probability mass over alternatives. This perspective unifies many existing SFT variants as implicit choices of the target distribution QQ. Building on this view, we propose Target-SFT which constructs the training objective directly from the desired target distribution. This method consistently outperforms across the ten reasoning dataset-model settings evaluated, showing the effectiveness of this target-based approach. Overall, our formulation reveals a more fundamental design principle for SFT training and opens a broader search space for SFT objectives.

1 Introduction

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a central stage in the post-training of large language models (LLMs) Zhang et al. (2025b); Chung et al. (2022); Ouyang et al. (2022). By imitating expert behaviors, SFT enables the pretrained model to acquire new knowledge and adapt to tasks efficiently. Despite its popularity, standard SFT relies on a particularly rigid form of supervision, by training the model toward a one-hot target distribution: at every token position, SFT maximizes the probability of the demonstrated token yty_{t}, while all other tokens are assigned zero probability. This formulation implicitly assumes that every observed token in the dataset is an ideal and uniquely correct target.

This one-hot view reveals a limitation of standard SFT, especially in post-training settings Gudibande et al. (2023); Li et al. (2025, 2026). In realistic SFT data, an observed token is rarely the only valid continuation. The same prompt may admit multiple correct reasoning paths, phrasings, intermediate steps, or stylistic choices Zhang et al. (2025a); Albalak et al. (2024); Yuan et al. (2023); Zhou et al. (2023); Liu et al. (2026); Wang et al. (2025). At the same time, the model already encodes a rich prior from pretraining Li et al. (2026); Wu et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2026). In such cases, forcing the model to strictly imitate every token can amplify noise, induce overconfidence, interfere with the pretrained model prior, and degrade generalization Chu et al. (2025); Shenfeld et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2026). A growing line of work relaxes the SFT supervision by modifying the objective, for example, through token-level importance reweighting Wu et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2026); Ruan et al. (2025); Diao et al. (2026) or regularization Li et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2026a, b). While these approaches are effective, they are often presented as separate algorithmic choices. It remains unclear the connection between variants and how to construct better SFT objectives.

In this work, we propose a different perspective: rather than the loss, we ask what target distribution should SFT drive the model to learn. This is more fundamental than the choice of loss alone, because loss is merely an optimization surrogate, while the target distribution directly specifies the desired allocation of probability mass (Figure 1). By viewing SFT as target distribution design, we can control the supervision signal when the observed label yty_{t} is suboptimal: If yty_{t} is ideal and unique, the target should be close to the one-hot distribution δyt\delta_{y_{t}}, maximizing its probability. If it is noisy or misaligned with the model prior, then the target should soften supervision and allocate probability to alternatives. Building on this intuition, we introduce a QQ-target distribution framework for SFT:

Qt=γt​δyt+(1−γt)​π~t,\displaystyle Q_{t}=\gamma_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-\gamma_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t},

where γt∈[0,1]\gamma_{t}\in[0,1] controls the target probability assigned to the observed token yty_{t} based on uncertainty, and π~\tilde{\pi} specifies the plausible alternatives for the 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} residual probability mass. This perspective thus reveals two key questions through the choices of (γt,π~t)(\gamma_{t},\tilde{\pi}_{t}): (1) how much to rely on the observed token yty_{t}, and (2) where to allocate the remaining probability mass when yty_{t} is uncertain?

In particular, we show that many existing SFT variants can be understood as varying ways of answering these two questions, and seemingly different losses correspond to implicit choices of target distribution QQ. Based on this insight, we propose Target-SFT by explicitly leveraging the structure revealed by the QQ-framework, which previous methods have largely left implicit. In general, we argue that the fundamental object in SFT is not the loss function itself, but the target distribution induced by the loss. This QQ-target perspective provides a unifying lens in SFT objective design, and exposes a general design space for balancing dataset imitation, prior preservation, and alternative supervision. Our contributions are as follows:

  1. 1.

    We introduce a target-distribution perspective on SFT, showing that arbitrary token-level SFT losses can be understood through the induced target distributions they drive the model to learn.

  2. 2.

    We propose the QQ-target framework, which unifies existing SFT variants by decomposing objective design into two explicit choices: how much to rely on the observed token and how to allocate the remaining probability mass.

  3. 3.

    We propose Target-SFT as a concrete instantiation motivated by the QQ-target view, and empirically validate its performance across 10 dataset-model settings.

2 Related Work

Existing works improve SFT along three main directions. We organize them under the QQ-target lens, which characterizes SFT variants by how they specify the effective token-level target distribution. We include the concrete connection for representative methods in Appendix C.

Token-level Reweighting.

Standard SFT applies uniform cross-entropy updates to all tokens, treating every token as equally reliable. Token-reweighting methods challenge this assumption by changing how strongly each token contributes to training. DFT Wu et al. (2026), beyond-log Li et al. (2026), and ProFit Liu et al. (2026) use the model probability on the observed token to rescale or filter updates, focusing on tokens that are compatible with the model prior. EAFT Diao et al. (2026) uses entropy-based uncertainty to reduce potentially destructive updates; iw-SFT Qin and Springenberg (2025) and CFT Ruan et al. (2025) assign weights based on trajectory- or token-level quality. These methods primarily address the choice of γt\gamma_{t} in SFT target construction, controlling how much target mass should be assigned to the observed label. However, reweighting the one-hot loss only weakens or strengthens imitation, but leaves the remaining probability mass underspecified. Our framework provides a complete view by making the effective training target explicit.

Distribution-level Prior.

Another line of work introduces soft distributional signals beyond the one-hot label. Reference-constrained methods such as ASFT Zhu et al. (2026a), RL’s Razor Shenfeld et al. (2025), and Proximal SFT Zhu et al. (2026b) regularize updates to prevent large drift from a reference model. Huang et al. Huang et al. (2025) uses label-smoothing to address overconfidence. GEM Li et al. (2025) uses reverse KL and entropy regularization to preserve output diversity and reduce forgetting. These methods address the rigidity in strict one-hot imitation, proposing alternative sources for probability allocation. They mainly specify the choice of π~\tilde{\pi}, allocating probability mass to non-observed alternatives: KL-constraints allocate toward the reference model, label smoothing allocates toward a uniform prior, and diversity-preserving methods discourage collapse onto a narrow set of tokens.

Dataset-level Curations.

A complementary direction improves SFT by changing the training trajectories. Prior work has proposed augmenting demonstrations with multiple valid trajectories Yuan et al. (2023); Yu et al. (2024), filtering examples by quality Chen et al. (2024a); Singh et al. (2024), and using model-generated or model-aligned responses Chen et al. (2025). GRAPE Zhang et al. (2026) selects trajectories with high likelihood under the model, while rejection-sampling fine-tuning trains on correct model-generated responses Yuan et al. (2023); Chen et al. (2024b); Zelikman et al. (2022); Xiong et al. (2025). Self-distillation fine-tuning Yang et al. (2024) projects expert demonstrations into the model’s distributional style, reducing data-model mismatch. These approaches address the same underlying issue as our work: the demonstrated trajectory or token may not be uniquely ideal for the model to imitate. By improving the dataset, they indirectly change the effective target distribution seen during SFT. In contrast, our work remains on the objective level and directly designs the effective target distribution for a fixed dataset.

3 Preliminary

Supervised Fine-Tuning.

Let 𝒟\mathcal{D} be a supervised dataset of pairs (x,y)∼𝒟(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}, where xx is the input prompt and y=(y1,…,yT)y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{T}) is the demonstrated response sequence. Given the prefix xt=(x,y<t)x_{t}=(x,y_{<t}), a language model defines the next-token distribution πθ(⋅∣xt)∈Δ|𝒱|\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|} over the vocabulary 𝒱\mathcal{V}.

Standard SFT minimizes the token-level negative log-likelihood:

ℒSFT​(θ)=𝔼(x,y)∼𝒟​[−∑t=1Tlog⁡πθ​(yt∣xt)].\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x_{t})\right].

Target Distribution.

Equivalently, let δyt\delta_{y_{t}} denote the one-hot vector that assigns probability 11 to the observed token yty_{t} and 0 to all other tokens, δyt​(v)=𝟏​{v=yt}\delta_{y_{t}}(v)=\mathbf{1}\{v=y_{t}\}. Then the objective can be written as the cross-entropy with target δyt\delta_{y_{t}} as ℒSFT(θ)=𝔼(x,y)∼𝒟∑t=1TCE(δyt,πθ(⋅∣xt)).\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathrm{CE}\bigl(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\bigr).

4 Q-Target Framework for SFT

The one-hot target δy\delta_{y} in SFT implicitly assumes that yty_{t} is the single optimal continuation for the prefix xtx_{t}. However, an observed token can be non-unique, noisy, or distributionally mismatched with the model prior. To capture this, we relax the assumption and account for the uncertainty in yty_{t}, constructing a new target distribution QtQ_{t} in place of δy\delta_{y}.

4.1 Modeling Latent Trust

We introduce a latent binary variable to represent whether the observed token should be strictly imitated. Let Zt∈{0,1}Z_{t}\in\{0,1\} be where Zt=1Z_{t}=1 indicates yty_{t} is strictly trusted as the target, and Zt=0Z_{t}=0 indicates that supervision should relax to a broader distribution over plausible alternatives. Under this view, the ideal target distribution can be written as

P(⋅∣xt)=P(Zt=1∣xt)δyt+P(Zt=0∣xt)π~t(⋅∣xt),\displaystyle P(\cdot\mid x_{t})=P(Z_{t}=1\mid x_{t})\delta_{y_{t}}+P(Z_{t}=0\mid x_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t}(\cdot\mid x_{t}), (1)

where π~t∈Δ|𝒱|\tilde{\pi}_{t}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|} denotes an alternative distribution over plausible next tokens.

Since ZtZ_{t} is unobserved, the trust probability rt=P​(Zt=1∣xt)r_{t}=P(Z_{t}=1\mid x_{t}) is unknown. We model this uncertainty using a Beta distribution:

rt∼Beta​(αt,βt),\displaystyle r_{t}\sim\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_{t},\beta_{t}),

where αt\alpha_{t} is evidence supporting yty_{t}, and βt\beta_{t} is evidence that yty_{t} may be non-unique or should relax toward alternatives. The posterior mean γt=𝔼​[rt]=αtαt+βt∈[0,1]\gamma_{t}=\mathbb{E}[r_{t}]=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}}\in[0,1] then gives the expected trust for the observed token yty_{t}.

Taking expectation over Eq. (1) leads to the ideal target distribution

Qt=𝔼rt[P(⋅∣xt)]=γtδyt+(1−γt)π~t.\displaystyle Q_{t}=\mathbb{E}_{r_{t}}\!\left[P(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right]=\gamma_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-\gamma_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t}. (2)

Intuitively, the target probability for yty_{t} is scaled by the expected trust γt\gamma_{t} in the token, and the residual probability mass is reallocated to plausible alternatives over π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}.

4.2 Final Target & Objective

We replace the SFT one-hot target with QtQ_{t}, and train with the cross-entropy loss:

ℒQ(θ)=𝔼(x,y)∼𝒟∑t=1TCE(Qt,πθ(⋅∣xt)).\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Q}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathrm{CE}\!\left(Q_{t},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right).
Proposition 1 (Token-level decomposition of QQ-target training).

Given the target distribution QtQ_{t} defined in Eq. (2), the token-level cross-entropy loss at position tt decomposes as

CE(Qt,πθ(⋅∣xt))=γtCE(δyt,πθ(⋅∣xt))+(1−γt)CE(π~t,πθ(⋅∣xt)).\displaystyle\mathrm{CE}\!\left(Q_{t},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right)=\gamma_{t}\,{\color[rgb]{0.82421875,0.41015625,0.1171875}\mathrm{CE}\!\left(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right)}+(1-\gamma_{t})\,{\color[rgb]{0.1171875,0.390625,0.70703125}\mathrm{CE}\!\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t},\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right)}. (3)

This shows that training to match the QQ-target involves two forms of supervision: (1) label imitation, which pushes the model toward the observed token yty_{t}, with strength controlled by the expected trust γt\gamma_{t}, and (2) residual distribution matching, which assigns the remaining supervision mass to alternatives through π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}. 5 Unifying Perspective

5.1 Existing Variants

The QQ-target formulation separates token-level supervision into two design choices: (1) γt∈[0,1]\gamma_{t}\in[0,1] controls the target probability mass on yty_{t}, while (2) π~t∈Δ|𝒱|\tilde{\pi}_{t}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|} specifies how the residual probability is allocated. We show that this view unifies many existing SFT variants. Table 4 provides details of each variant discussed, and Table 5 summarizes their interpretation under QQ-target framework.

Standard SFT.

Consider the degenerate choice γt=1\gamma_{t}=1, then the QQ-objective in Eq. (3) reduces to the negative log-likelihood in SFT, corresponding to setting Qt,k=δytQ_{t,k}=\delta_{y_{t}}:

CE​(Qt,πθ)\displaystyle\mathrm{CE}(Q_{t},\pi_{\theta}) =CE​(δyt,πθ)=−log⁡πθ​(yt∣xt),\displaystyle=\mathrm{CE}(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta})=-\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x_{t}), Qt​(k)\displaystyle Q_{t}(k) =δyt​(k)={1,k=yt,0,k≠yt.\displaystyle=\delta_{y_{t}}(k)=\begin{cases}1,&k=y_{t},\\ 0,&k\neq y_{t}.\end{cases}

Hence, standard SFT is the special case that places full probability on every observed token yty_{t} and assigns no residual mass to alternatives.

Token-Weighted Variants.

A class of SFT variants modifies the objective by scaling the negative log-likelihood with a detached, per-token importance weight wtw_{t} Li et al. (2026); Liu et al. (2026); Wu et al. (2026); Ruan et al. (2025); Diao et al. (2026); Qin and Springenberg (2025):

ℒt=−wt​log⁡πθ​(yt∣xt),\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}=-w_{t}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x_{t}),

where wtw_{t} may depend on model confidence, entropy, sample quality, or other token-level statistics.

Corollary 1 (Token weighting as self-residual QQ-target.).

Assume wt∈[0,1]w_{t}\in[0,1] is detached from the current update. The token-weighted loss above corresponds to the choice

(γt=wt,π~t=sg[πθ(⋅∣xt)])⟹Qt=wtδyt+(1−wt)sg[πθ(⋅∣xt)].\displaystyle\left(\gamma_{t}=w_{t},\,\tilde{\pi}_{t}=\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right]\right)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q_{t}=w_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-w_{t})\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right].

where sg​[⋅]\mathrm{sg}[\cdot] denotes the stop-gradient operator. In particular, the residual branch π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t} is a self-matching term that contributes no gradient. See Appendix B.2 for proof.

This shows that token-weighted variants primarily specify γt\gamma_{t}, proposing statistics to determine how strongly to imitate the observed token. And the residual mass 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} is allocated to the current model prior sg[πθ(⋅∣xt)]\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right], providing no corrective supervision toward potential alternatives.

Distributional Variants.

Another class of SFT variants incorporates distributional signals beyond the observed token Li et al. (2025); Huang et al. (2025); Zhu et al. (2026a, b); Gu et al. (2026). With various intended goals (e.g., to regularize model drift, calibrate confidence, preserve output diversity, etc), these methods enrich hard-label imitation using another distribution target qtq_{t}. The objective is of the form

ℒt=−at​log⁡πθ​(yt∣xt)+bt​CE​(qt,πθ),at,bt≥0.\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}=-a_{t}\log\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x_{t})+b_{t}\,\mathrm{CE}(q_{t},\pi_{\theta}),\qquad a_{t},b_{t}\geq 0. (4)
Corollary 2 (Distributional variants as residual Q-targets).

Given a detached, auxiliary or reference distribution qt∈Δ|𝒱|−1q_{t}\in\Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|-1}, distributional variants correspond to QQ-target training with

γt=atat+bt,π~t=qt,⟹Qt=γt​δyt+(1−γt)​π~t,\displaystyle\gamma_{t}=\frac{a_{t}}{a_{t}+b_{t}},\qquad\tilde{\pi}_{t}=q_{t},\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q_{t}=\gamma_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-\gamma_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t}, (5)

up to a global constant at+bta_{t}+b_{t}. Therefore, these methods primarily specify the residual branch π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}, deciding where non-label probability should be allocated. The relative strength γt\gamma_{t} between label imitation and residual matching is determined by fixed hyperparameter. See Appendix B.3 for proof.

In summary, token-weighted variants mainly design the label-trust coefficient γt\gamma_{t}, while distributional variants design the residual distribution π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}. Together, they present the two axes in our framework: how strongly to imitate the observed token, and how to allocate the remaining probability mass.

Remark.

This is a natural view because an SFT loss is a training surrogate. Although variant losses may take different algebraic forms, their effect on the model is mediated through the probability update over the vocabulary. The loss expression is therefore only a way to generate gradients; the induced target distribution reveals what those gradients effectively drive the model to match. In this sense, the QQ-target formulation is a more fundamental perspective beyond the loss forms. This view not only unifies variants but provides a direct lens into training signals. We now formalize this idea, where we derive the induced target QtQ_{t} for any arbitrary differentiable token-level loss.

5.2 From Any Loss to QQ

An SFT loss defines a surrogate for shaping the model’s next-token distribution. At each prefix xtx_{t}, the model outputs a distribution pt=πθ(⋅∣xt)p_{t}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t}), and the loss produces gradients that determine how this probability changes across tokens. Therefore, for any differentiable token-level loss, we can ask what target probability distribution QtQ_{t} does the loss drive the model to match through its gradients?

Given token position tt (notation omitted for clarity), consider the cross-entropy toward target QtQ_{t}

ℒCE​(Q,p)=−∑k∈𝒱Qk​log⁡pk.\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(Q,p)=-\sum_{k\in\mathcal{V}}Q_{k}\log p_{k}.

Let zz denote the logits. The gradient with respect to the kk-th logit is simply the prediction difference

∂ℒCE∂zk=pk−Qk.\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\partial z_{k}}=p_{k}-Q_{k}.

This relationship can be inverted. Given any differentiable token-level loss ℒ​(z,x)\mathcal{L}(z,x) with logit gradient gk=∂ℒ∂zkg_{k}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial z_{k}}, we can derive its induced target as

Qk:=pk−gk.\displaystyle Q_{k}:=p_{k}-g_{k}. (6)

This QQ is the target whose cross-entropy gradient ∇zCE​(Q,p)=∇zℒ\nabla_{z}\mathrm{CE}(Q,p)=\nabla_{z}\mathcal{L}, aligning exactly with the logit updates produced by ℒ\mathcal{L}. It therefore explicitly reveals the training signal encoded by the loss. Appendix D shows example derivations, and visualizes loss through its gradients and target QQ.

6 Target-SFT

The QQ-formulation turns SFT supervision from a static log-likelihood objective into a problem of target distribution design: (1) how much to rely on the observed token yty_{t}, and (2) how to allocate the remaining probability mass. We now introduce Target-SFT, which leverages both branches of this construction. We first use a model-based proxy to estimate label uncertainty, and motivate for an external teacher distribution to enrich supervision signals through the residual branch.

Probability-Proxy for 𝜸𝒕\bm{\gamma_{t}}.

The ideal target in Eq (2) involves an expected trust γt=𝔼​[rt]=αtαt+βt\gamma_{t}=\mathbb{E}[r_{t}]=\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha_{t}+\beta_{t}}, where αt,βt\alpha_{t},\beta_{t} represent evidence (such as an empirical count) for the binary event yty_{t} being selected given prefix xtx_{t}. However, such a count is intractable in SFT.

Instead, the model probability py=πθ​(yt∣xt)p_{y}=\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}\mid x_{t}) arises as a natural proxy for αt\alpha_{t}, which encapsulates statistical evidence accumulated during pretraining. Here pyp_{y} represents the fraction of the model’s belief assigned to yty_{t} among all possible continuations. By defining the evidence as αt=py\alpha_{t}=p_{y} and βt=1−py\beta_{t}=1-p_{y}, the posterior mean resolves to

γt\displaystyle\gamma_{t} =pypy+(1−py)=py.\displaystyle=\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+(1-p_{y})}=p_{y}.

This derivation motivates probability-weighted SFT variants (such as pp-loss) Wu et al. (2026); Li et al. (2026), as scaling the SFT target by pyp_{y} equates to using predictive probability as proxy measure of uncertainty in the label.

Teacher-Guided Reward Shaping 𝝅~\bm{\tilde{\pi}}.

Under the QQ-target view, such objectives implicitly use a self-matching residual, π~t=sg[πθ(⋅∣xt)],\tilde{\pi}_{t}=\mathrm{sg}\!\left[\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t})\right], as derived in Section 5.1. This shows that as py→0p_{y}\rightarrow 0, the supervision weakens and provides no further corrective gradient. This is limiting because a small pyp_{y} may arise from uncertainty in label, or indicate the lack of knowledge where SFT is intended to teach. A purely self-prior residual treats both cases the same, by reducing the imitation strength and providing no additional guidance over plausible alternatives.

This motivates a residual distribution that remains anchored to the model prior, but also allows external corrective signals. To this end, we construct a teacher-guided π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}. The goal is to preserve model prior without being constrained by it, enabling supervision from teacher-supported alternatives.

We construct π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t} through KL-regularized reward shaping:

π~=arg⁡maxq∈Δ⁡[𝔼a∼q​[r​(a)]−τ​KL​(q∥πθ)],\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}=\arg\max_{q\in\Delta}\left[\mathbb{E}_{a\sim q}[r(a)]-\tau\mathrm{KL}(q\|\pi_{\theta})\right],

which stays close to πθ\pi_{\theta} while the reward rr specifies the alternative tokens to be upweighted. Let πT(⋅∣xt)\pi_{T}(\cdot\mid x_{t}) denote a teacher distribution. To incorporate teacher guidance, we define reward using the teacher log-probability, r​(a)=λ​log⁡πT​(a)r(a)=\lambda\log\pi_{T}(a).

The solution has the form π~​(a)∝πθ​(a)​exp⁡(r​(a)/τ)\tilde{\pi}(a)\propto\pi_{\theta}(a)\exp(r(a)/\tau), and substituting the teacher reward yields

π~​(a)∝πθ​(a)​πT​(a)η,η=λ/τ.\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}(a)\propto\pi_{\theta}(a)\pi_{T}(a)^{\eta},\qquad\eta=\lambda/\tau.

This results in a teacher-guided π~\tilde{\pi} close to πθ\pi_{\theta}, while upweighting alternatives favored by the teacher through token-level reward. For easier interpretation, we consider the closely related form

π~tguided​(a)∝πθ​(a∣xt)1−η​πT​(a∣xt)η,η∈[0,1].\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\mathrm{guided}}(a)\propto\pi_{\theta}(a\mid x_{t})^{1-\eta}\pi_{T}(a\mid x_{t})^{\eta},\qquad\eta\in[0,1].

It interpolates between the student (η→0\eta\rightarrow 0) and teacher distribution (η→1\eta\rightarrow 1). This parameterization is convenient in practice, since η\eta directly controls the intensity of teacher signals.

Target-SFT.

Combining the probability-proxy for trust estimate γt=py\gamma_{t}=p_{y} and the teacher-guided residual distribution π~=π~guided\tilde{\pi}=\tilde{\pi}_{\text{guided}} gives the following target:

QtTarget=py​δyt+(1−py)​π~tguided.\displaystyle Q_{t}^{\textsc{Target}}=p_{y}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-p_{y})\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\mathrm{guided}}. (7)

The corresponding token-level objective decomposes as

CE​(QtTarget,πθ)=py​CE​(δyt,πθ)+(1−py)​CE​(π~tguided,πθ).\displaystyle\mathrm{CE}\!\left(Q_{t}^{\textsc{Target}},\pi_{\theta}\right)=p_{y}\,\mathrm{CE}\!\left(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta}\right)+(1-p_{y})\,\mathrm{CE}\!\left(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{\mathrm{guided}},\pi_{\theta}\right). (8)

Target-SFT adaptively balances strict imitation and prior preservation. When the observed token yty_{t} is well-supported by the model, the objective approaches standard SFT. When yty_{t} is uncertain, it weakens one-hot fitting and instead assigns a higher weight to the teacher-guided residual branch. In this regime, teacher supervision acts as a fallback supervision that (1) avoids overfitting to uncertain labels, and (2) strengthens signals when a desired token is under-supported by student due to low pyp_{y}.

7 Experiments

7.1 Setup

For mathematical reasoning, we train on two datasets: NuminaMath-CoT-67k LI et al. (2024); Li et al. (2026) and OpenR1-Math-15k Bakouch et al. (2025); Yan et al. (2025). For broader scientific reasoning, we train on m23k Huang et al. (2026), a high-quality medical reasoning dataset. Our experiments cover across seven diverse models: Qwen2.5 (1.5B & 7B), Qwen2.5-Math (1.5B & 7B), Qwen3-1.7B-Base, LlaMA-3.2-3B, LlaMA-3.1-8B.

We compare Target-SFT against the following baselines: (1) SFT, which trains with the standard negative log-likelihood. (2) SFT (pp-loss) Li et al. (2026); Wu et al. (2026), a probability-weighted variant that scales loss by the model probability on the observed token. (3) Knowledge Distillation Hinton et al. (2015), which applies teacher distribution on the data as supervision. We use the standard form ℒDistill=c​CE​(πT,πθ)+(1−c)​CE​(δyt,πθ)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Distill}}=c\,\mathrm{CE}(\pi_{T},\pi_{\theta})+(1-c)\,\mathrm{CE}(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta}), where cc is constant and ablated in Table 6. These baselines correspond to different partial choices in the QQ-target design space: SFT uses the one-hot target, SFT (pp) uses γt=pyt\gamma_{t}=p_{y_{t}}, and KD uses the full teacher distribution as a fixed distributional signal. Target-SFT considers both (γt,π~t)(\gamma_{t},\tilde{\pi}_{t}) and adaptively balances label imitation and the residual branch based on uncertainty in label.

For models that involve teacher distribution, we use the corresponding instruction-tuned model as the teacher; for example, Qwen2.5-1.5B uses Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. For the Qwen3 series, we use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the teacher. The evaluation performance is measured by Average@16 accuracy. Further details of evaluation and training configurations are provided in Appendix A.

7.2 Main Results

Across all evaluations, Target-SFT achieves the highest Average@16 accuracy. Figure 2 summarizes the results across tasks. While the baselines each show complementary strengths and weaknesses in different dataset-model settings, Target-SFT consistently gives the best results. This highlights the value of the structure exposed by the QQ-target framework: effective SFT can arise from carefully designing both the imitation strength and the allocation of remaining probability mass.

Math.

Table 1 reports the results on NuminaMath-CoT and OpenR1-15k. Target-SFT achieves the highest average accuracy across all models on both datasets. In contrast, standard SFT yields only modest gains over the base model in many cases, and even hurts performance for Qwen3-1.7B on NuminaMath-CoT (14.26→12.9914.26\rightarrow 12.99). This is consistent with prior observations that rigid one-hot matching can be limiting for mathematical reasoning Li et al. (2026); Yuan et al. (2023); Yu et al. (2024); Zelikman et al. (2022). The relatively strong performance of probability-weighted SFT (pp-loss) further suggests that reducing imitation strength on uncertain tokens is beneficial. However, Target-SFT improves over the pp-loss in every case. This supports our claim that choosing γt\gamma_{t} alone and defaulting the residual mass to model prior is incomplete. By explicitly designing π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}, Target-SFT enhances the supervision and achieves stronger performance.

Direct distillation, which uses teacher distribution as the full target, performs relatively weakly on mathematical reasoning. On NuminaMath-CoT, distillation is only slightly better than standard SFT in some cases, such as Qwen3-1.7B-Base (17.8117.81 vs. 12.9912.99) and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (25.7625.76 vs. 23.8823.88), whereas Target-SFT outperforms significantly. This suggests that simply replacing the target with a soft teacher distribution is still suboptimal. In contrast, Target-SFT uses the teacher to shape the residual branch, whose weight 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} increases when yty_{t} is under-supported. This adaptive use of teacher signals proves to be more effective than full distillation in these settings.

Medical.

Table 2 shows a different pattern on medical reasoning. The gap between standard SFT and pp-loss is smaller than in math, and in some cases standard SFT is clearly stronger. For example, on Qwen2.5-1.5B the two methods achieve similar averages, while on LLaMA-3.1-8B standard SFT substantially outperforms pp-loss (47.4147.41 vs. 38.6038.60). This suggests that for some tasks, stricter imitation is more effective, perhaps because the demonstrations align more closely with the desired answer distribution. In such cases, the pp-loss that uniformly weakens supervision for all low-pyp_{y} tokens is not ideal. Nevertheless, Target-SFT still achieves the best average performance across all models on the medical setting. This shows that the teacher-guided residual branch remains valuable even when probability-based reweighting alone is less effective. Distillation is also more competitive on medical reasoning than on math, outperforming pp-loss on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B. However, Target-SFT still outperforms distillation across all models. The mixed performance of distillation again suggests that teacher information is useful, but treating it as a fixed full target is not always the best approach. Under the QQ-target design, the teacher instead acts as adaptive fallback supervision, which supplements corrective signals for missing knowledge while preserving the model prior.

7.3 Ablation Study.

We ablate the two key design choices in Target-SFT. We include results on varying (1) the intensity of teacher supervision in the residual distribution, controlled by η\eta. This tests the effect of the teacher model and the method’s sensitivity to this hyperparameter e​t​aeta. We further vary (2) the adaptive weighting of the residual branch, controlled by 1−γt1-\gamma_{t}. This ablation directly tests whether using an uncertainty-dependent residual weight 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} is effective, compared to simply assigning a fixed constant weight cc to the π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t} branch. Additionally, we also tune the hyperparameter cc for distillation to explore potential performance gain for the baseline.

We report the full results in Appendix E. In summary, the ablation studies validate both components of Target-SFT. While varying η∈{0.2,0.5,1.0}\eta\in\{0.2,0.5,1.0\} gives averages in the range from 34.3034.30 to 38.0538.05, they all outperform the baselines and the best distillation setting (34.3034.30 vs. 30.8330.83). In contrast, further tuning of the hyperparameter c∈{0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0}c\in\{0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0\} for the distillation baseline does not lead to gains beyond the result presented in Table 1, but instead fluctuates significantly from 22.8122.81 to 30.8330.83. The ablation on residual weight 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} also shows the effectiveness of this design, where changing to this to a constant cc degrades the performance.

8 Conclusion

In this work, we show that supervised fine-tuning is fundamentally a target distribution design. We formalize this view through the QQ-target framework Qt=γt​δyt+(1−γt)​π~tQ_{t}=\gamma_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-\gamma_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t}, which exposes two hidden design choices: how strongly to imitate the observed token, and how to allocate residual probability mass over alternatives. This lens unifies many existing SFT variants as implicit choices of γt\gamma_{t} or π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}. Building on this insight, we present Target-SFT and empirically demonstrate its effectiveness across ten reasoning data-model settings. Overall, our results offer a novel and more complete perspective into SFT, and open a broader design space for future SFT methods.

Appendix A Experiment Details

Training Configurations.

All SFT experiments are conducted using verl, largely following the work by Li et al. [20]. The optimizer used is AdamW, with the learning rate of 5×10−55\times 10^{-5} for all models. We use cosine decay scheduling with a warm-up ratio of 0.1. We use the maximum sequence length of 3072 for both mathematical reasoning datasets. The global train batch size is 256, with gradient accumulation. The experiments are conducted on A6000 and H200 GPUs. All models are trained for 1 epoch. Table 3 summarizes all training and evaluation-related configurations.

Teacher Models.

For methods that involve teacher distribution, we use the corresponding instruction-tuned model as the teacher for the base model; for example, Qwen2.5-1.5B uses Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. For Qwen3-1.7B-Base, we use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 as the teacher. For Target-SFT, we choose the teacher signal intensity from η∈{0.2,0.5,1.0}\eta\in\{0.2,0.5,1.0\}. To ensure consistency across methods, we cache all teacher logits before training and use the same cache for both distillation and Target-SFT experiments. For memory efficiency, the cached teacher distribution is truncated to the top-64 tokens in the vocabulary with the highest probabilities.

Datasets.

For NuminaMath-CoT which originally contains 859859k chain-of-through problems, we following Li et al. [20] and use the 6767k subset organized in their work. For the OpenR1 training dataset, we sample 1515k from OpenR1-Math-46k-8192 [37], which contains verified traces generated by DeepSeek-R1 for problems collected from NuminaMath 1.5 [21]. We collect the responses with sequence length shorter than 30723072 to standardize with the training configuration for NuminaMath-CoT. For scientific reasoning, we use m23k [15], a 2323k high-quality medical reasoning dataset.

Evaluation.

For mathematical reasoning, evaluation covers five representative benchmarks: Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, AIME24, AMC23, and Math500 [19, 16, 25, 24, 12]. For models trained on m23k, we evaluate on the same benchmarks following Li et al. [20], which include MedMCQA [27], MedQA-USMLE [17], PubMedQA [18], MMLU-Pro [34], GPQA (Medical) [29], Lancet & NEJM [15], MedBullets [3], and MedXpertQA [48]. Evaluation follows the same protocol as Li et al. [20] and Huang et al [15]. Inference uses a maximum generation length of 40964096 tokens with the decoding temperature 1.0. For distillation and Target-SFT on Qwen2.5-Math-7B trained from the longer-sequence OpenR1-15k dataset, we use the decoding settings with temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.8 as recommended for its teacher model Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct. The reported results are averaged over 16 generations for every prompt.

Some distributional methods fall outside the nonnegative residual-mixture form in Eq. (4). For example, GEM [22] discourages model from concentrating high probability on the strongest non-label alternatives. This can be expressed schematically as a repulsive branch π~t,τ−\tilde{\pi}^{-}_{t,\tau} as follows

π~t,τ−​(k)=πθ​(k∣xt)1/τ∑v∈𝒱πθ​(v∣xt)1/τ,τ<1.\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{t,\tau}^{-}(k)=\frac{\pi_{\theta}(k\mid x_{t})^{1/\tau}}{\sum_{v\in\mathcal{V}}\pi_{\theta}(v\mid x_{t})^{1/\tau}},\qquad\tau<1.

This represents a τ\tau-sharpened model distribution, which places mass on high-probability tokens. And its objective is conceptually written as

ℒt=CE​(δyt,πθ)−λ​CE​(π~t,τ−,πθ),\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{t}=\mathrm{CE}(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta})-\lambda\,\mathrm{CE}(\tilde{\pi}_{t,\tau}^{-},\pi_{\theta}),

shaping the residual branch repulsively against collapsing onto a small set of tokens, thereby aligning with GEM’s goal of preserving diversity in SFT.

This can be viewed as a signed residual extension of the Q-target framework, with π~t=(π~t+,π~t−)\tilde{\pi}_{t}=(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{+},\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{-}). This defines a desired and undesired target alternative, where Qt=δyt+π~t++π~t−Q_{t}=\delta_{y_{t}}+\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{+}+\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{-}. Instead of matching a positive residual distribution, GEM specifies an undesired residual direction to preserve diversity. More generally under this view, distributional variants can shape the residual branch to emphasize or suppress particular subsets of tokens.

Appendix C Unifying Framework

In this section, we provide concrete connections between SFT variants discussed in Section 2 and our QQ-target perspective. Table 4 shows the loss formulation and core motivation for each variant. Following the formulation Qt=γt​δyt+(1−γt)​π~tQ_{t}=\gamma_{t}\delta_{y_{t}}+(1-\gamma_{t})\tilde{\pi}_{t}, these methods can be interpreted through their choices of γt\gamma_{t} and π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t}, as summarized in Table 5.

We organize existing methods into three broad categories. Label-trust variants modify the imitation strength on yty_{t}, corresponding to different choices of γt\gamma_{t}. Residual-distribution variants primarily specify where the non-label probability mass should go. In these cases, the corresponding γt\gamma_{t} is often obtained by normalizing the relative weights of the hard-label and residual branches, with any overall scale absorbed into the effective learning rate. Finally, data-level methods do not directly alter γt\gamma_{t} or π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t} for a fixed demonstration. Instead, they reshape the empirical target distribution by selecting, filtering, or rewriting the training trajectory y^\hat{y}, after which standard one-hot SFT is applied. We therefore include them as indirect instances of target distribution design at the dataset level.

In particular, GEM [22] can also be illustrated with a signed extension of the QQ-framework, where the residual component contains both positive and negative branches, π~t=(π~t+,π~t−)\tilde{\pi}_{t}=(\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{+},\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{-}). This defines a desired and undesired target alternative, where Qt=δyt+π~t++π~t−Q_{t}=\delta_{y_{t}}+\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{+}+\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{-}. And GEM utilizes π~t−\tilde{\pi}_{t}^{-} as a repulsive branch to discourage probability collapse onto a small set of high-probability tokens, hence preserving diversity. Proximal SFT [47] is included as a schematic connection, since its clipping-based trust-region objective is not an explicit residual target but implicitly constrains updates.

Throughout the table, (γt,π~t)(\gamma_{t},\tilde{\pi}_{t}) are treated as stop-gradient unless otherwise specified. Some methods are originally defined at the sequence or trajectory level [28, 30], we present their token-level decompositions or analogues to make the connection to QtQ_{t} explicit.

Appendix D From Any Loss to QtQ_{t}

Section 5.2 introduces the derivation of QQ-target from any differentiable token-level SFT loss. We now provide two examples and visualize their effects below.

Example 1: Standard SFT.

Standard SFT minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the observed token ℒSFT​(z)=−log⁡py,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(z)=-\log p_{y}, where pyp_{y} is the model probability assigned to yy. The logit gradient is

gj=∂ℒSFT∂zj=pj−𝟏​{j=y}.\displaystyle g_{j}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}}{\partial z_{j}}=p_{j}-\mathbf{1}\{j=y\}. (12)

Substituting this into Eq. (6), we obtain the induced target

QSFT​(j)=pj−gj={1,j=y,0,j≠y.\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{SFT}}(j)=p_{j}-g_{j}=\begin{cases}1,&j=y,\\ 0,&j\neq y.\end{cases} (13)

This recovers exactly the one-hot target distribution δy\delta_{y}, assigning all target mass to the observed token and zero mass to alternatives.

Example 2: Probability-Weighted SFT.

Consider the detached probability-weighted loss

ℒp-loss​(z)=−sg​[py]​log⁡py,\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{p-loss}}(z)=-\mathrm{sg}[p_{y}]\log p_{y}, (14)

This loss has the logit gradient

gj=sg​[py]​(pj−𝟏​{j=y}).\displaystyle g_{j}=\mathrm{sg}[p_{y}](p_{j}-\mathbf{1}\{j=y\}). (15)

Substituting this gradient into Eq. (6), and using the scalar sg​[py]=py\mathrm{sg}[p_{y}]=p_{y} derives the induced target

Qp-loss​(j)={2​py−py2,j=y,(1−py)​pj,j≠y.\displaystyle Q_{\text{p-loss}}(j)=\begin{cases}2p_{y}-p_{y}^{2},&j=y,\\ (1-p_{y})p_{j},&j\neq y.\end{cases} (16)

This closed form reveals the mechanism of probability-weighted SFT. When the model assigns high probability to the observed token (py→1p_{y}\rightarrow 1), the induced target approaches the one-hot SFT target. When the model is uncertain (py→0p_{y}\rightarrow 0), the induced target relaxes toward the model’s own distribution Qj→pjQ_{j}\rightarrow p_{j}. Thus, low-confidence tokens receive weaker imitation updates, and the residual probability mass defaults to the student prior. In the QQ-target notation, this corresponds to choosing γ=py\gamma=p_{y} and π~=sg​[πθ]\tilde{\pi}=\mathrm{sg}[\pi_{\theta}], thereby preserving the model prior when evidence for strict imitation on yy is weak.

Appendix E Ablation Study

We ablate on the two key design choices in Target-SFT: (1) intensity of teacher supervision in the residual distribution, controlled by η\eta, and (2) adaptive weighting of the residual branch, controlled by 1−γt1-\gamma_{t}. The first ablation tests the effect of teacher model and the method’s sensitivity to this hyperparameter η\eta. The second ablation tests whether using an uncertainty-dependent residual weight 1−γt1-\gamma_{t} is useful, compared to simply assigning a fixed constant weight cc to the π~t\tilde{\pi}_{t} branch.

Additionally, we ablate on the hyperparameter c={0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0}c=\{0.2,0.5,0.8,1.0\} in the knowledge distillation baseline (ℒDistill=c​CE​(πT,πθ)+(1−c)​CE​(δyt,πθ))\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Distill}}=c\,\mathrm{CE}(\pi_{T},\pi_{\theta})+(1-c)\,\mathrm{CE}(\delta_{y_{t}},\pi_{\theta})\bigr). The results in Table 6 show that Target-SFT still achieves the highest results among all baselines. On the other hand, knowledge distillation in this setting is highly sensitive to the mixture weight cc. Overall, the ablations validate both components of Target-SFT, showing that using a teacher-guided prior as a fallback mechanism in the residual branch is highly effective.

Appendix F Teacher Model Alignment

This section analyzes the alignment the model πθ\pi_{\theta} and teacher distribution πT\pi_{T} to better understand the effects of teacher guidance. Figure 4 visualizes the conditional distribution P​(pT∣pθ)P(p_{T}\mid p_{\theta}) of the teacher probability pT=πT(⋅∣xt)p_{T}=\pi_{T}(\cdot\mid x_{t}) given the policy model probability pθ=πθ(⋅∣xt)p_{\theta}=\pi_{\theta}(\cdot\mid x_{t}), both evaluated on the observed ground-truth training token yty_{t}.

A large portion of tokens lies near the diagonal, where both models assign similar probabilities to yty_{t}. However, the distribution shows meaningful spread around the diagonal. This indicates useful correction signal, since even modest deviations from the diagonal are cases where the teacher’s confidence differs and thus providing signals on alternative tokens in vocabulary. While the visualization only shows the marginal probabilities on yty_{t} for clarity, we note that the more important effect is the teacher’s full redistribution of probability mass across alternative tokens. And divergence on yty_{t} serves as a proxy for for broader differences in the teacher’s beliefs over the vocabulary, enabling fine-grained adjustments that the student’s current distribution cannot capture.

The off-diagonal density in Q1 represents stronger disagreements (high pTp_{T}, low pθp_{\theta}), where teacher guidance is most informative. These tokens correspond to cases where the base model lacks confidence, but the teacher recognizes the token as plausible or important. This shows the role of teacher guidance as a fallback signal: when the student is uncertain, the teacher strengthened supervision rather than relying solely on the student’s current belief. Notably, many tokens cluster at pT≈1.0p_{T}\approx 1.0, indicating that the teacher frequently provides strong corrections on tokens the student underweights.

In Q2 (both pTp_{T} and pθp_{\theta} low), both models are uncertain about the observed token. These may correspond to noisy, idiosyncratic, or highly non-unique tokens. Standard SFT treats them as fully reliable labels, forcing exact imitation through one-hot target δyt\delta_{y_{t}}. In contrast, a softer target reduces imitation strength here, allowing probability mass to plausible alternatives. This helps avoid fitting dataset artifacts or arbitrary surface choices.

In Q3, the two models assign high probability to yty_{t}. These are high-confidence tokens where the label is likely reliable and unambiguous. The target thus remains close to standard SFT one-hot δyt\delta_{y_{t}}, since both models support strong imitation. Finally, in Q4 (high pθp_{\theta}, low pTp_{T}), the student model is already confident but the teacher is uncertain. In this case, the teacher supervision is downweighted through a smaller 1−γt1-\gamma_{t}, naturally reducing its influence.

Together, this analysis supports the motivation of Target-SFT, providing a selective fallback distribution to adjust the confidence on dataset tokens. It strengthens supervision when the model is uncertain, while relaxing imitation on low-confidence uncertain tokens. This provides a more adaptive alternative to standard SFT, which imposes the strict one-hot target δyt\delta_{y_{t}} regardless of token uncertainty or model-data alignment. Appendix H Response Length

This section analyzes the relationship between the model’s output response length and its accuracy performance. Figure 8 shows the comparison on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B for the two mathematical reasoning datasets, and Qwen2.5-1.5B for the m23k dataset.

On m23k and NuminaMath-67K, trained models that produce longer responses generally have higher accuracies than the shorter-output SFT variants, suggesting that the increase in reasoning length is beneficial. However, this relationship between response length and model performance is not consistent on OpenR1-15K, where standard SFT produces the longest responses but achieves substantially lower accuracy than the others. Meanwhile, Target-SFT achieves the highest accuracy with relatively shorter responses. This indicates that this pattern may be dataset-dependent and response length alone is not a reliable proxy for reasoning quality.

A closer inspection of model outputs suggests that the long responses from the base model often reflect poor generation behavior rather than longer reasoning. In particular, the base model occasionally shows rambling, unstable formatting, repetition, or failure to terminate properly. This inflates the average response length while yielding weak performance. In contrast, all trained models produce more consistent outputs and more stable response formats.

Standard SFT appears especially sensitive to the surface style of the training corpus. For example, on OpenR1-15K, it tends to imitate the dataset’s long chain-of-thought monologue style, closely matching its response length and tone. This observation aligns with the intuition behind SFT’s one-hot target δyt\delta_{y_{t}}, which applies strict supervision to every observed token in the training data. As a result, standard SFT may overfit to dataset-specific surface forms, including verbosity and stylistic structures, without necessarily improving the robustness of the underlying reasoning process.

By comparison, SFT(pp), distillation, and Target-SFT tend to preserve more of the base model’s flexibility. Their outputs fall into a more moderate-length regime and avoid the extreme verbosity of standard SFT. These methods appear to trade some of SFT’s confident dataset-matched style for a more consistent reasoning structure that transfers better across corpora, leading to stronger accuracy without simply increasing response length or mimicking styles from the dataset.
